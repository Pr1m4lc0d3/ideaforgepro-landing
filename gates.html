The seven gates

Every gate separates the bloom from the slag.

Founders build first and find out last — six months of code, then a search for someone to pay for it. Most ideas die in the market, not in the code. Each gate has a graduation test. Fail one and the forge makes you fix it before you go on, because a weak buyer poisons every answer that follows.

01 — Buyer

A buyer type sharp enough to go and find — “owners of single-location coffee roasters”, not “small businesses” — who already pay for something here, and you can say where they gather. The named individual comes later, at the test.

02 — Problem

In their words, not yours, with a reason to act now rather than next year. If they are in pain: frequent, and already worked around. If they buy this because they want it, the honest answer is the appetite they already spend on — an invented ache fails here.

03 — Offer & price

The outcome they are buying, at a number they say yes to without a meeting. Defensible against doing nothing.

04 — Value artifact

Something real you can hand over before the next thing gets built — and that does the selling while you sleep.

05 — Pre-build test

A costly, revealed commitment — a real dollar, a signature — with the pass line and the kill line written down before you start. Never a poll or a waitlist. Free product? Then name who eventually pays, and when.

06 — Channel

One channel, gone deep, aimed at people already showing intent. Not a list you bought.

07 — Builder spec

Only now: the minimum build that delivers the promise. Cold, hard, and ready to hand to whoever writes it.
